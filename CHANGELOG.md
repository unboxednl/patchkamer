# Changelog

*Nederlandse versie: [CHANGELOG_NL.md](CHANGELOG_NL.md)*

All notable changes to Patchkamer (the host and the desktop app) per version. The version number is in `patchkamer/version.json` and shown under **Settings > Version**. Older history is in the git log.

## 0.21.0 (2026-11-09)

### Added
- **Clip Arranger 1.26.0: managing scenes, multiple songs and stretching clips.**
  - **Scenes:** the number is free (1 to 24, was fixed at 8). Per scene: rename (double-click the name works too), insert an empty scene, duplicate (all tracks) and delete; drag scenes to reorder them. **+ Scene** adds one at the end and **Delete empty scenes** removes empty scenes that are in no song. Deleting a scene removes the sections that point to it (in every song) and **Undo** brings everything back. The **Edit scene names** drop-down is gone.
  - **Multiple songs on the same scenes:** above the song bar a song picker with **Song name**, **New song**, **Duplicate song** (a remix starts as a copy) and **Delete song**. A song is only the order of sections; scenes and clips are shared. Dragging a scene onto a song block assigns it to that section, onto the **+** in the song bar makes a new section at the end. Scenes that are not in the chosen song are dimmed and the tooltip shows which songs do use them. The field at the top is now called **Project**.
  - **Stretching clips over scenes:** drag the handle at the bottom of a clip down over empty scenes (or fill in **Across scenes** in the clip editor). Scene k then plays from k/count of the clip, when launching scenes and in the song (with or without **Continue**); a covered cell shows *part 2/4* and a non-empty cell stops the stretch.
  - **Dragging clips is visible in every colour scheme:** all possible targets get a dashed outline and the target under the mouse a thick outline with a fill, in the scheme's own colours (before, it was only clear in dark).
  - The matrix scrolls inside its frame with fixed track headers and a fixed scene column, so 24 scenes do not make the page longer.
  - Earlier songs and presets work unchanged (`arrangement` stays the active song). Measured: the 58 presets and the default project give the same state and exactly the same events and times as 1.25.0, in song and session mode. Not listened to. Manual (NL and EN) and device notes updated.

## 0.20.0 (2026-11-09)

### Added
- **Manual window.** The menu behind the icon on the card bar now has **Manual** on every device. It opens the manual of that device in a floating window inside Patchkamer itself (no new tab), with the pictures. The text comes from the docs folder on GitHub (`docs/<kind>/USER-MANUAL_NL.md` or `_EN.md`); the language follows the host language and English is the fallback, also when a heading is missing. If GitHub cannot be reached, the window tries the file next to the host. You only see the part for the device; **Whole manual** shows the whole kind, and links to other manuals open in the same window. The window can be dragged and resized, and on a narrow screen it is a sheet at the bottom. The markdown is converted without `innerHTML`, so a manual cannot run a script.

## 0.19.0 (2026-11-09)

### Added
- **Ports in the title bar and a menu item About {device} (contract v1.33, step 1).** A device's ports now sit as dots in the title bar (between the name and the version select) instead of in a row of their own, which makes each device about 40 px shorter. This does not apply to narrow devices (up to and including 340 px wide), the Mixer, devices that draw their own ports and devices with more than eight ports; a skin with its own port mode takes precedence. The menu behind the icon on the card bar has **About {name}**, with the explanation from the new, optional `about` field of the device (a text or a text per language). The contract (NL and EN, §12g) and the validator know `about` and say that a device no longer repeats its own name in its UI; the test set gained six cases (476). The devices themselves are not changed yet: that follows in the next steps (duplicate titles out, explanations into `about`).

## 0.16.0 (2026-11-09)

### Added
- **Skin editor: type, colour at rest and colour in use per control (skins contract 0.8, device contract v1.32).** Select a single knob, fader or pad in the editor and set only its look. A **knob or slider** gets a *colour at rest* and a new *colour in use*, the colour the pointer, the filled part or the thumb takes while you press or drag it (`activeColor` in `design.controls`). A **pad** gets a **type**: *On/off* (stays on until you press again), *Pad (no velocity)* (sounds while you press) or *Pad (with velocity)* (the host measures how hard you press: the pressure on a touch screen, with a mouse the place of the click, top soft and bottom hard), a *colour at rest* (lightly tinted), a *colour in use* (on or pressed) and an *LED* (`design.pads.rules`, per id, role or group). The face of a control (rotary, horizontal or vertical slider) was already per control and stays.
- **The type is the first thing in a skin that changes what a control does.** It works on pads that the host draws (`host.ui.pad`); pads a device draws itself and step buttons get the colours but keep their behaviour. A pad that is latched when the skin takes the type away is released. A device receives the velocity as a second argument: `onpress(e, velocity)` and `onchange(on, velocity)` (1 to 127, 100 by default), so a device that ignores it sounds the same.
- **Contract.** Skins contract 0.8 (`activeColor`, `pads.rules`) and device contract v1.32 (the `velocity` argument), both in Dutch and English; the skins validator checks the new fields (+16 test cases).

## 0.13.0 (2026-11-09)

### Added
- **Pad Sampler 1.5.0** (phase 2b): three new things.
  - **Time-stretch** (tab Edit, **Process**, next to Tempo match): change the tempo without changing the pitch (to a target BPM using the beat count, or by a factor of 0.5 to 2) or the pitch without changing the tempo (semitones and cents, at most 12). The method is WSOLA in the time domain with two settings: **Rhythmic** (short windows; every attack is detected and starts exactly in its place, so drums stay sharp) and **Tonal** (long windows, for tone and melody). The result is stored in the sample (selection or the whole sample), is one Undo step and obeys the 64 MiB and 120 s limits. The work runs in a background thread (Blob worker) with progress and a Cancel button; where the browser does not allow that it runs in slices on the main thread. Listen first with **Listen**.
  - **SoundFont import (SF2 and SF3)** in the tab Banks (Local): choose a `.sf2` or `.sf3` file, pick a **preset or instrument** from the list and see the zones and memory before loading. Only the tables are read up front; only the samples of the chosen preset are read from the file and decoded, so a large file does not have to fit in memory. Taken over: key and velocity ranges (with preset and instrument ranges intersected), root note, coarse and fine tuning (and the sample correction), loops, volume envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release) as AD or ADSR, pan, attenuation, filter cutoff and resonance (roughly, low-pass), exclusive classes as mute groups and linked left and right samples as one stereo sample. A percussion preset (bank 128) becomes drum pads, over up to four banks. The report lists what was ignored (generators and modulators). The licence comes only from the INFO block of the file (copyright and comment) and is shown; SF3 samples are decoded with the browser decoder.
  - **XPM program import** (`.xpm`, XML) in the tab Banks: drum and keygroup programs with instruments and layers, sample name, key and velocity range, root note, tuning, level, pan, loop, one-shot, mute groups, polyphony and volume envelope; the samples are looked up by name among the files or the folder you choose, as with SFZ. Unknown parts are ignored and counted in the report. Written from public descriptions and tested only with home-made files: check the result.
  - The state gets no new fields: old states load unchanged and `setState(getState())` stays exact. Importers can now fill up to 128 zones (SoundFont and XPM; SFZ and Decent Sampler keep 16).

## 0.10.0 (2026-11-08)

### Added
- **AI question for devices (contract v1.29).** A device can ask the AI provider chosen in the settings one question with `host.ai(this, 'ask', …)`; the key stays with the host, the user confirms per device and the answer is untrusted text. ## 0.8.0 (2026-11-08)

### New
- **New MIDI design: MIDI Controller and Keyboard, and retired devices** (contract v1.28). The **MIDI Controller** is one device for all your MIDI controllers and keyboards: all connected inputs work at once, you choose which Patchkamer device you control, and with a button on your controller you switch to another device; the same knobs then work for that device. Optionally the selected device is put in the middle of the screen. Mapping is quick: **Learn layout** (once), **Suggest** (puts the knobs on the controls automatically) and **Quick map**. ## 0.7.0 (2026-11-08)

### New
- **Compressor** (new device, Effects category): a compressor in an AudioWorklet with threshold, ratio from 1:1 to infinite, knee from hard to 24 dB soft, attack and release as time constants (plus an automatic release with two stages), make-up (plus automatic make-up), peak or RMS detection, lookahead up to 10 ms, a highpass and lowpass on the sidechain, an external sidechain input, parallel mix, stereo link and mid/side, listening to the sidechain, a reduction meter and a transfer curve with the live level; 30 presets in the pack Compressor Basics.

- **De-esser** (new device, Effects category): tames harsh s sounds in an AudioWorklet, with a detection band from 3 to 10 kHz (bandwidth in octaves), threshold, ratio, maximum reduction, attack and release, two modes (split-band reduces only the band, wideband the whole signal), listening to the detection band, parallel mix, a reduction meter and a frequency display; 21 presets in the pack De-esser Basics.

- **Bitcrusher** (new device, Effects category): bit depth from 1 to 16 (fractions allowed) and sample-rate reduction (hold or linear, with jitter) in an AudioWorklet, with four quantiser styles (mid-tread, mid-rise, truncate, µ-law), dither (RPDF, TPDF, shaped), noise, an anti-alias pre-filter you can switch off so aliasing is a choice, high-pass and low-pass after the crush, mix and level, and a small screen showing the quantised waveform; 25 presets in the pack Bitcrusher Basics.
- **Delay** (new device, Effects category): an extended delay in an AudioWorklet. The time is free (1 to 4000 ms), synced to the host clock (note values from 2 bars to 1/64, straight, dotted or triplet, or a number of sixteenths such as 3/16 and 5/16) or based on an own tempo set with Tap; left and right can differ, with a stereo offset. Modes: stereo, ping-pong, mono and dual (own feedback per side), plus cross feedback. Feedback goes up to 120% with a soft limiter in the loop; filters (high-pass and low-pass), tape or tube saturation and diffusion sit in the loop; modulation (sine, tape, chorus), ducking from its own input, freeze, reverse, host swing on the synced time, and a tempo or time change either as a click-free crossfade or as a deliberate tape pitch glide. The card shows the current time in ms and note value, and the host tempo is also followed while the clock is stopped. 35 presets in the pack Delay Basics.
- **Reverb** (new device, Effects category): an algorithmic reverb with five models (Hall, Plate, Spring, Room, Chamber) in one AudioWorklet. Controls: size, decay (RT60), pre-delay (free or to tempo with note values), high damping and low decay, diffusion, modulation, early/late balance, input high-pass and low-pass, stereo width, ducking, freeze and mix. Measured offline: the decay time follows the setting within about 5%, the dry path has no latency, and the tail stays finite for NaN, infinity and 1e30 bursts; not listened to. 31 presets in the pack Reverb Rooms (Hall, Plate, Spring, Room, Chamber, Creative).
- **Convolution Reverb** (new device, Effects category): a convolution reverb with 15 built-in impulse responses (church, concert hall, studio room, booth, tiled bathroom, stairwell, cave, tunnel, steel plate, spring tank, metal tube, metal bin, gated burst, reverse swell, water tank) that are generated in code, so the device file holds no audio. You can also load your own impulse response with **Load file** or from the **Samples** tab; the audio never ends up in a preset. Controls: pre-delay, start and length (trim), stretch, early/tail balance, high-pass, low-pass, tilt, width and mix; a change of impulse response crossfades, so it does not click. Latency 0 samples; not listened to. 30 presets in the pack Convolution Spaces.
- **Pitch Shifter** (new device, Effects category): shifts the pitch by −24 to +24 semitones plus ±100 cents at the same tempo, in an AudioWorklet with three engines (Percussive grains with 1 to 3 ms latency for drums, Grain with a correlation search for voices and instruments, and a Vocoder with phase-locked peaks, formant keeping and formant shift), three window sizes, a dry/wet mix that is time-aligned, a feedback with tone filter and soft limiting for shimmer and octave echoes, a glide time, and the latency shown on the card; 30 presets in the pack Pitch Shifter Basics.
- **Harmonizer** (new device, Effects category): up to four voices from one input, each with its own interval (semitones, or scale steps inside a chosen key and scale), level, pan, detune and delay, in an AudioWorklet with a grain or a vocoder engine (formant keeping); the scale steps follow the pitch of the input (monophonic detection) or the notes on a new event input, with a glide time and an optional humanize; 23 presets in the pack Harmonizer Basics.
- **Pitch Correction** (new device, Effects category): monophonic pitch correction in an AudioWorklet: YIN pitch detection (about 62 to 1100 Hz, three selectable ranges), correction to the nearest note of twelve per-pitch-class switches (with key and scale as a shortcut), retune speed from 0 ms (hard) to 400 ms, amount, vibrato keeping, adjustable tuning of A, a threshold and a hold time for unvoiced parts (they pass through), a grain engine (11 to 14 ms latency) or a vocoder with formant keeping (21 to 85 ms), and a display of the heard and corrected note; 24 presets in the pack Pitch Correction Basics.

## 0.6.0 (2026-11-08)

### Added
- **Pad Sampler 1.2.0: chop, Process and Flatten** (step 3 of the device). In the **Edit** tab you now choose the kind of edit **Edit**, **Chop**, **Process** or **Flatten**. **Chop** makes slices (markers in the sample, not copies) with equal parts, by threshold, by hand or by BPM and bars; the markers are numbered flags on the waveform that you drag with mouse and finger (with snap to zero crossing), the start and end of a slice are linked or separate, and you listen to a slice with a click or the keys 1 to 9 and the arrows. **Slices to pads** puts slice n on pad n with the same sample (start and end per layer, optionally a tail of 100 ms). **Process** really saves filter (also as a sweep), drive, bitcrusher, three-band EQ, compressor and tempo match (resampling to a target BPM, with BPM from the number of beats) in the sample, with a listen first. **Flatten** renders a pad (all layers, envelope, filter, tune, level and pan) or the selection with an offline render to a new sample, at a chosen note and velocity, as a new layer, replacing layer, replacing pad or on another pad; reverb and delay are not baked in. Everything can be undone in the same history and stays within 64 MiB. The state gets an optional field `sliceEnds` per sample; old states load unchanged.

## 0.5.0 (2026-11-08)

- **Pad Sampler 1.1.0: waveform editor and sample edits** (step 2 of the device). - **Stem Splitter** (new device, Tools category, contract v1.23): splits a track into stems (vocals, drums, bass, the rest, sometimes more) with an online service, and plays them with the studio clock, each stem on its own output with level, mute and solo. Pick a file or drop it on the card, use the **→ Stem Splitter** button in the Samples tab, or record from the audio input. The sound goes to the service on the internet, which costs money per track, and you need the rights to the music: the card and a question the first time per session say so, with the estimated cost, and nothing is sent until you click **Split** (cancelling is possible). The service is chosen in the device and its key, model and price are entered under **Settings › Stem splitting**; the key stays only in this browser. Supported: fal.ai with Mureka (zip of stems, optional MIDI) or Demucs (4 or 6 stems), and an **own service** with the same queue protocol. The number of outputs follows the number of stems, and an extra bundle output carries all stems to the Multitrack or Mixer. Save the stems as WAV (16 or 24 bit, per stem or all in a zip) and hand the drums to the **Drum Listener**. The host gets a new call, `host.stems` (the device itself never uses the network). A WAV, FLAC or OGG file (or a recording) is converted to mp3 on your computer when the service only accepts mp3 or m4a (the card says so; 192 kbps, stepping down to 64 kbps to stay under 10 MB, about 20 minutes at most in stereo; encoder lamejs, loaded only when needed). - **Drum Listener** (new device, Tools category): listens to a drum loop and writes down the pattern. It finds the hits, sorts them into kick, snare (clap, rim), closed and open hat, toms and percussion, works out tempo, swing and grid (1/16, 1/32, triplets) and shows a lane grid you can edit. Send it with the clock to a drum machine (General MIDI notes or trigs), or copy it with the Copy button and paste it into the Pattern Tracker, Clip Arranger or Step Sequencer. Everything runs on your computer, with no online service. Measured on our own drum loops, not on real recordings and not listened to: kick and hats are recognised well, the snare fairly well; open hat, rim, clap, toms, percussion and anything with bass or chords under it are weak.
- **Tune Finder** (new device, Tools category): listens to a sound (one note, a short phrase, a bass line or a chord of one instrument; an audio file or a recording from the input) and finds the synth and tune of the library that comes closest, as a list of tunes and of instruments. **Load** puts the chosen tune on that synth in the workspace. Every tune of the synths (2211 tunes of 34 instruments) was played offline on four notes and measured; the measurements sit inside the device (about 1.6 MB), rebuilt with `docs/tools/build-tune-index.py` when tunes are added. - **Bass Finder** (new device, Tools category): listens to a monophonic bass line (a bass, sub or synth bass, no chords; an audio file or a recording from the input) and writes down the notes: pitch, start, length and velocity. It tracks the pitch from 27 to 500 Hz with octave-error protection, cuts the notes on pitch jumps and attacks (slides and short staccato notes included), estimates tuning, tempo, swing and downbeat and puts the notes on a grid of 1/8, 1/16, triplet 1/16 or off. Correct the notes on a piano roll (move, octave up and down, length, add, delete), **Copy** them as a clip and **Paste** them into a sequencer, or send them with the clock to a synth such as Low Bass. Measured on random bass lines rendered with Patchkamer's own bass synths (not used for tuning): F1 about 0.95 for clean sound and 0.92 over ten situations (soft, noisy, low-passed, mono, staccato, legato, glide, chorus); with a lot of room reverb about 0.80. Not tested on real recordings and not listened to.
